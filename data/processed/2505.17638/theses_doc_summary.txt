This study investigates the training dynamics of diffusion models to understand how they avoid memorizing training data and achieve generalization. Through empirical experiments and theoretical analysis of a random features model, the authors identify two distinct training timescales: an early time ($\tau_{gen}$) when models generate high-quality samples, and a later time ($\tau_{mem}$) when memorization begins. Crucially, they find that while $\tau_{gen}$ remains constant, $\tau_{mem}$ increases linearly with the training set size, creating a widening window of training times where early-stopped models generalize effectively. The authors conclude that this implicit dynamical regularization prevents memorization even in highly overparameterized settings, offering practical strategies like early stopping to train robust generative models.
